### Common problems I find

Most small business sites don't need more content to rank. They need the basics fixed. These are the issues I see most often:

- **The site is hidden from Google.** A single WordPress setting, "Discourage search engines", is left on after launch and blocks the whole site.
- **Pages aren't indexed.** Google has crawled them but chosen not to show them, often because of thin or duplicate content.
- **Duplicate and missing titles.** Every page shares the same title, or has none, so Google can't tell them apart.
- **Broken links and lost pages.** A redesign changed URLs without redirects, and the pages that used to rank now return errors.
- **Slow pages.** Oversized images and too many plugins drag down Core Web Vitals, especially on phones.
- **Wrong business details.** Opening hours, service areas or addresses differ between the site, Google Business Profile and directories.
- **No structured data.** Google has to guess what the business is, where it works and what it offers.

### What the audit covers

#### Crawling and indexing

I check robots settings, your XML sitemap, canonical tags, noindex tags and Google Search Console's coverage report, so we know which pages Google can reach and which it actually shows.

#### Duplicates and thin content

I find pages that compete with each other, copied text across pages, and pages too thin to rank. For each one I recommend whether to merge, rewrite or remove it.

#### On-page SEO

Titles, meta descriptions, headings, image alt text and internal links, checked page by page against the keyword each page should target.

#### Search performance

Using Search Console data, I show which searches already bring impressions, which pages are close to page one, and where the quickest wins are.

#### Speed and mobile

Core Web Vitals, image sizes, plugin load and how the site behaves on a phone.

#### Local and business details

Name, address and phone consistency, service areas, opening hours and structured data for your business.

### What you get

- A written report in plain English, not a raw tool export
- Every issue ranked as critical, high or medium, with the reason it matters
- A fix plan in order: what to do first and what can wait
- A short call to walk through the findings

### Fixes, done safely

You can take the report to anyone, or I can make the fixes. When I do, I back up the site and test changes on a staging copy before they go live. Typical fixes include:

- Turning indexing back on and requesting re-indexing in Search Console
- Writing unique titles and meta descriptions for every page
- Setting up Yoast SEO, sitemaps and structured data
- Redirecting broken and old URLs
- Compressing images and removing unneeded plugins
- Correcting business hours, service areas and contact details across the site

After the fixes go live, I check them on the live site and in Search Console, not just in a preview.
